What does a 1-10 "probability" mean in a sector risk register?

A sector register for supply chain due diligence must let a user compare sectors on one protected right. A score from 1 to 10 can do that only if the register says what the score measures. This study tests that idea on public data and shows where each step works and where it fails.

Independent study by Nikhil Sehgal. Working draft, built 2026-11-16. It is not a product of Catena-X, BMW Group or Ulm University. It uses public data only. All numbers on this page come from the scripts in the code repository.

Core idea

Define the score.Each score needs an event, a unit, a period and a published rule that turns a rate into a point. Without these, the same data give very different scores.
Do not count mentions.Recorded and reported cases follow the reporting system. A sector with no reports is not a sector with no risk.
Show the uncertainty.Each entry has a range and a confidence grade, so a reader can see if a score comes from much data or from little.
Measure the AI.Rules read the fixed parts of a report. A language model reads free text only where rules cannot, and a program tests each quotation.

B. Local language model

Model: models/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-Q4_K_M.gguf, temperature 0.

Known-answer test. The model read the raw pages with the lists of identified risks. The rules of stage A give the correct answer. 35 lists with 202 risk entries: the model found 199, missed 3 and added 43 that are not in the list (precision 0.822, recall 0.985). 25 of 35 lists are identical to the rules.

  • The author compared the 10 lists that differ with the report text. The rules were correct in each of them.
  • In 5 of these 10 lists the company answered "Keine" (none). 40 of the 43 added entries come from these lists. In 4 of the 5 lists the model gave exactly the risks that the company lists for a different scope.
  • Reading: where the layout is fixed, rules are more reliable than this model. The typical error of the model is not an invented risk. It is a real risk in the wrong place.

Free text. 66 descriptions of prioritised risks. The model names the industry, supplier group, product group, raw material or activity that the company connects to the risk, selects a NACE division and gives a quotation. A program tests if the quotation and the group are in the description word for word. Each design runs with German and with English instructions.

4. Integration

How does the sector score combine with the country register and the company size?

Limits

  • One right with real rates. Public rates per NACE division exist for accidents at work. For child labour and forced labour no such rates were found, so the register on this page says nothing about them.
  • EU data. The rates describe work in the EU, not global supply chains.
  • Likelihood only. Severity is a separate dimension and is not scored.
  • The four questions are an assumption. They are the reading of the author from public documents. Nobody at Catena-X, BMW Group or Ulm University confirmed them.
  • No comparison with other tools. The scores are not compared with existing sector risk databases or with expert judgement.
  • Text pipeline. 14 reports of large German companies for one year, one small language model, and no check of the model output by a person yet.
  • Stability is not validity. The tests show that the score is stable over time. They do not show that it is correct.
